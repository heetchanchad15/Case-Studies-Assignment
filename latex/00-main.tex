\begin{abstract}
  This report examines the role of Machine Learning Engineer in Trust and Safety
  Engineering, using a live graduate advertisement at TikTok as its anchor. The
  professional problem the role exists to solve is triage under a fixed review budget: a
  platform receives far more user-generated content each day than any moderation team can
  read, so a classifier must rank items by the probability of violation and spend scarce
  human attention where it changes an outcome. Two public corpora of crowd-labelled user
  text are used to study that problem --- 24,783 annotated tweets and 159,686 annotated
  Wikipedia Talk comments --- and two machine learning algorithms, a linear support vector
  machine and a feed-forward neural network, are compared under stratified cross
  validation. Because the removal-worthy class covers only 5.8\% and 9.6\% of the two
  corpora respectively, the evaluation deliberately avoids accuracy in favour of
  precision, recall, area under the precision-recall curve, and precision over the slice
  of the ranked stream a fixed review team could actually process. The analysis finds that
  the two data sources are complementary in what they teach about model choice but
  directly contradictory in what they teach about labels, and that this contradiction is
  invisible to any single-dataset evaluation.
